Zone 2 Cardio: How to Train Your Aerobic Base

Zone 2 is the intensity at which you can still hold a conversation, and it is the foundation almost everyone skips. It feels too easy to count, so people drift upward into moderate effort where they are too tired to train often but not working hard enough to gain much. Do it properly and it improves almost everything: heart function, fat oxidation, recovery between hard efforts, and how much training you can tolerate.

Why this works

Below your first lactate threshold, your body burns primarily fat, uses slow-twitch fibres and recovers quickly — so you can accumulate a lot of it without ruining the rest of your week.

Set-up

  1. Choose something repeating and low-skill: walking, cycling, rowing, elliptical. Zone 2 requires 30–60 minutes of uninterrupted rhythm. Avoid anything you have to think hard about.
  2. Estimate your ceiling: roughly 180 minus your age, or 60–70% of your max heart rate. These are estimates, not gospel. The talk test is more reliable than a wrist-worn heart rate monitor.
  3. Set up the talk test as your real guide. You should be able to speak in full sentences with mild effort. If you cannot, you are above zone 2.
  4. Plan to be slightly bored. If it feels satisfyingly hard, it is intervals, not base work. Boredom is the signature of a correct pace.

How to do it, step by step

  1. Start at 25–30 minutes, three times a week

    Frequency beats duration at first. Three short sessions are easier to recover from and easier to keep doing.

  2. Hold a pace where your breathing is deep but nasal or barely mouth-based

    If you can only breathe through your mouth heavily, back off. If you can sing, go faster.

  3. Add five minutes a week to one session

    Over eight weeks that takes you from 30 minutes to around 55 without ever feeling like a jump.

  4. Once a week, do 45–60 minutes instead of two shorter sessions

    The long easy session is where most of the aerobic adaptation accumulates.

  5. Every fourth week, reduce the volume by 30%

    Deloading prevents the cumulative fatigue that makes easy cardio stop being easy.

Common mistakes (and how to fix them)

Drifting into the grey zone

The most common error. If your session leaves you needing a nap, you went too hard. Slow down and add time instead.

Chasing a heart rate number that is wrong for you

Wrist optical sensors lag and are inaccurate during intervals; formulas are population averages. Use perceived effort as the primary guide.

Nothing but zone 2, forever

Base work is the foundation, not the whole house. Pair it with one or two strength sessions and, optionally, one interval session.

Coaching cues & progressions

  • Inclined walking is the most underrated zone 2 modality: joint-friendly, easy to do in a lunch break, and no equipment.
  • Use podcasts or audiobooks as a training tool — if you cannot follow the plot, your intensity is too high.
  • Heart rate drift is normal in the heat or when dehydrated. Judge effort, not the number.
  • Do ze 2 work after strength training on the same day, never before. Never let easy cardio compromise your lifts.

Safety notes

If you have a heart condition, uncontrolled blood pressure or are returning after illness, get medical clearance before starting a regular cardio programme. Do not turn easy cardio into moderate cardio. Time, not intensity, is the variable you add. If you have an existing injury, are pregnant, or are returning to exercise after a long break, get clearance from a doctor or physiotherapist before starting.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know I am really in zone 2?

The talk test: full sentences, slight effort, could hold it for an hour. If you have a chest strap and want precision, zone 2 is roughly 60–70% of max heart rate, or the point just below where breathing becomes noticeably harder.

Is walking enough?

Brisk walking on a slight incline absolutely counts, especially for beginners. Faster modalities make it easier to accumulate 3–4 hours a week.

How long until I notice a difference?

Four to six weeks for resting heart rate and everyday stamina; eight to twelve for measurable changes in aerobic capacity. It is slow, reliable progress.

Can I do zone 2 every day?

Yes — that is one of its main advantages. It is low enough intensity that most people can do it daily, which is exactly why it builds such a large base.

Does it help with fat loss?

It burns more fat per minute than hard intervals, but total calories matter more than the fuel source. Its real value for body composition is that it does not wreck your recovery, so you can train consistently.
